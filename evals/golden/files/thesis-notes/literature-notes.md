# Literature notes

## Marsh & Ilunga (2019), "Convenience beats conviction"
Study of 14 neighbourhood composting schemes. Main finding: distance to the bin mattered more than stated environmental attitudes. Participation fell by about 40% where the nearest bin was more than 300 m away. Weakness: only self-reported participation.

## Halvorsen (2021), social norms and visible bins
Visible, shared bins made neighbours' participation noticeable, which raised participation. Argues the bin is a "social signal" as well as infrastructure. Mixed-methods; I haven't noted the sample size.

## Brandt & Coelho (2020), Kleinwald case study
Single-town case study in Kleinwald. Scheme launched with a lot of enthusiasm; two-thirds of households still active after two years. Credits a volunteer "bin champion" on each street.

## Oduya (2022), incentives
Reviewed reward schemes (vouchers, compost giveaways). Incentives raised sign-ups but the effect faded within six months once rewards stopped.

## Petit (2023), review article
Systematic review of 31 studies. Strongest predictors of continued participation: convenience, smell/pest management, and feedback (telling people how much they composted). Notes most studies are short (under 12 months).

## Quist (2024), pasted abstract from PDF
Survey of 600 households in three towns. Found that households with a garden were less likely to use communal bins because they compost at home, which can look like "dropping out" in scheme records.
