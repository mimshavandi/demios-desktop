# Notes: screens, light and teenage sleep (for my EPQ essay)

## What I'm fairly sure about
- Teenagers' body clocks shift later during puberty. They naturally feel sleepy later at night and want to wake later. This is well established in sleep science, not just "being lazy".
- Most guidance says teenagers need roughly 8–10 hours of sleep a night.
- Bright light in the evening delays the release of melatonin, the hormone that signals it's time to sleep. Daylight in the morning helps reset the body clock earlier.

## Things that are less clear
- **Blue light from screens.** Lots of articles say blue light from phones is the main reason teens sleep badly. But the light from a phone is much dimmer than room lighting or daylight. Some lab studies found a delay of melatonin of only a few minutes from reading on a tablet. Size of the effect: probably small.
- **What people do on screens may matter more than the light.** Exciting games, group chats and notifications keep people awake and push bedtime later. This seems more convincing to me but I've mostly found survey studies, which can't show cause and effect.
- "Night mode" filters: mixed evidence. One study in my notes found no difference in sleep onset.

## Survey I ran (my year group, 64 responses)
- 52 of 64 (81%) use their phone in bed most nights.
- Average self-reported sleep on school nights: 6 h 50 min.
- Students who said they put their phone outside the bedroom (n = 9) reported 7 h 35 min on average.
- Caveat: tiny group, self-reported, and people who put their phone away might differ in other ways (stricter parents? earlier risers?).

## Ideas for the essay
- Argue that the "blue light" story is overstated and that habits and school start times matter more.
- Mention that later school start times have been trialled in some places, with mixed but mostly positive results (need a proper source!).
- Recommendation: phones charged outside the bedroom, morning daylight, consistent wake-up time at weekends.
