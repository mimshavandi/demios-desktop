# Pennyjar budgeting app: user interview notes (round 2)
Interviews 5–16 October 2026. Researcher: Tamsin Rook. 8 participants (U1–U8).

## Participants
- U1 student, 21, uses the app weekly
- U2 nurse, 34, shift worker, uses daily
- U3 freelancer (illustrator), 29, irregular income
- U4 retired teacher, 67, new user (2 weeks)
- U5 parent of three, 41, shares budget with partner
- U6 software tester, 26, lapsed user (stopped in summer)
- U7 small café owner, 52, uses it for personal money only
- U8 student, 19, new user

## Themes

### 1. Shared budgets are painful (U2, U3, U5, U7)
- U5 and partner both enter spending; they often enter the same purchase twice. "We had the weekly shop twice and thought we'd overspent."
- U2 wants to share only one category (household) with her flatmate, not everything.

### 2. Irregular income doesn't fit monthly budgets (U2, U3, U6)
- U3 is paid per project; some months nothing, some months three invoices. The monthly budget view makes her feel she's failing in empty months.
- U2's shift pay varies with overtime.
- U6 stopped using the app partly because "every month started in the red".

### 3. Bank import errors erode trust (U1, U4, U6, U8)
- Duplicated transactions after reconnecting the bank (U1, U6).
- U4 didn't understand why a refund appeared as income.
- U8: "If I have to check every line anyway, what's the point?"

### 4. Older and new users find the setup overwhelming (U4, U8)
- U4 gave up on categories in the first session; too many defaults (38 categories).
- U8 liked the "simple mode" idea when it was described.

## Feature requests (as said by participants)
- Share a single category with another person (U2, U5)
- "Smooth" income across months, or budget per pay cheque instead of per month (U3, U2)
- Mark a transaction as "duplicate" with one tap (U1, U6)
- Fewer starting categories, a simple mode (U4, U8)
- Export to a spreadsheet for the tax return (U3, U7)

## Things people liked
- The weekly summary notification (U1, U2, U5, U8)
- Savings "pots" with pictures (U5, U8)

## Quote to share with the team
U6: "I didn't stop because I stopped caring about money. I stopped because the app made me feel bad every first of the month."
