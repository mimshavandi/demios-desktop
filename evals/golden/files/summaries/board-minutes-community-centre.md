## 1. Welcome, quorum and declarations of interest

The Chair welcomed everyone and confirmed the meeting was quorate (six of seven trustees present).

Rafael Ortiz declared an interest in item 5 (window replacement), because Ortiz & Sons, the firm he co-owns, submitted one of the three quotes. It was agreed that Rafael would leave the room for the discussion and decision on item 5 and would not receive the papers for that item in future.

No other interests were declared.

## 2. Minutes of the meeting on 17 September 2026

Approved as a correct record. Proposed by Mei Tanaka, seconded by Dariusz Wolny.

Matters arising: the replacement of the fire door in the main hall (action from September) is complete. The lock on the bike shed is still broken; Hollis has chased the contractor twice and will escalate.

## 3. Centre Manager's report

Hollis Grant presented the quarterly report (Q3, July to September 2026).

- Room bookings were up 14% on Q3 2025. The main hall's occupancy averaged 71% across opening hours, the highest since the Trust started recording it.
- Café revenue for the quarter was €18,420.
- Two new regular groups started in September: a Tuesday morning toddler music group and a Thursday evening chair-yoga class for older residents. Both are fully booked.
- The lift was out of service from 3 to 10 October. It was repaired for €1,180. This was not covered by the maintenance contract, because the fault was caused by water coming in through the lift shaft roof. Hollis has asked the roofer to inspect the shaft roof.
- Volunteer numbers have fallen to 46, down from 52 in the spring. The café and the reception desk on Saturday afternoons are the hardest to staff.
- Two complaints from neighbours on Brook Lane about noise when the Friday youth disco finishes (see item 7).

Trustees thanked Hollis and the staff for a strong quarter. Mei asked whether the volunteer drop was linked to the new rota system; Hollis said exit conversations pointed mainly to people moving away or taking on paid work, not the rota.

## 4. Finance

Kwame Asante presented the Q3 management accounts.

- Income for Q3: €61,300. Expenditure: €58,900. Surplus for the quarter: €2,400.
- Energy costs were 22% higher than in Q3 2025, despite lower usage, because the fixed-price contract ended in June.
- Unrestricted reserves stand at €84,000, which covers about 5.1 months of running costs. The Trust's reserves policy sets a minimum of six months. Kwame warned that the reserves are now below the policy level and that this should be considered alongside item 5.

**Draft budget for 2027.** Kwame proposed:

- Hall hire rates to rise by 6% from 1 January 2027.
- The concession rate for local charities and residents' groups to stay frozen at €12 an hour.
- A new energy contract to be sought for a fixed period of two years.

Dariusz argued against the 6% rise, saying it would push some smaller groups out and that a 4% rise plus a fundraising push would be fairer. Lotte noted that the concession rate protects the smallest groups. After discussion, the proposal was put to a vote.

**Decision:** Hall hire rates rise by 6% from 1 January 2027; concession rate frozen at €12 an hour. Vote: 5 for, 1 against (Dariusz Wolny).

**Action:** Kwame to bring three energy contract quotes to the November meeting.

## 5. Replacement of the hall windows

*Rafael Ortiz left the room for this item.*

The single-glazed windows in the main hall are rotting and are a major source of heat loss. Three quotes were received for replacing all 14 windows:

| Firm | Price | Guarantee | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Haldorf Glazing | €38,500 | 10 years | 8 weeks |
| Nordlicht Fenster | €42,200 | 10 years | 20 weeks |
| Ortiz & Sons | €36,900 | 5 years | 6 weeks |

Trustees discussed the quotes. Ortiz & Sons was the cheapest, but the 5-year guarantee was considered too short for a building that will rely on these windows for decades. Nordlicht Fenster's lead time would push the work into the spring term, when the hall is busiest.

The Haldorf Heritage Fund has confirmed a grant of €25,000 towards the windows. The remaining €13,500 would come from reserves. Kwame noted this would take reserves down to about €70,500 (roughly 4.3 months of running costs). Trustees agreed the energy saving justified it and asked for a plan to rebuild reserves to six months by the end of 2028.

**Decision:** Accept the quote from Haldorf Glazing for €38,500. Funded by the €25,000 Heritage Fund grant and €13,500 from reserves. Work to take place during the February half-term, 16–20 February 2027, when the hall is closed. Unanimous (5 trustees voting).

**Action:** Hollis to confirm the order with Haldorf Glazing and tell regular hirers about the February closure by 15 November.
**Action:** Kwame to draft a plan to rebuild reserves for the January meeting.

*Rafael Ortiz returned to the meeting.*

## 6. Safeguarding

Mei Tanaka, the safeguarding lead, presented the annual review of the safeguarding policy. Changes are minor: updated contact details and a clearer process for reporting concerns raised by children directly.

Background checks for youth volunteers must be renewed every three years. Four volunteers are overdue. They may not help at youth sessions until their checks are renewed.

**Decision:** Updated safeguarding policy approved.
**Action:** Mei to make sure all four overdue background checks are renewed by 31 December 2026.

## 7. Friday youth disco: noise

Two households on Brook Lane complained about noise from young people leaving the Friday disco at 22:30. Hollis has spoken to the youth leaders, who were supportive. Options discussed: finishing earlier, staggered pick-up, and a volunteer at the side gate.

**Decision:** From November the disco will finish at 22:00 instead of 22:30, and a volunteer will be at the side gate at closing.
**Action:** Hollis to write to Brook Lane neighbours explaining the changes, by 6 November.

## 8. Café

Pia Lund joined for this item. She proposed a trial of "pay what you can" soup on Wednesdays, to help residents struggling with costs. Soup costs about €1.10 a portion to make. The local food bank will tell its clients about it.

Trustees asked how the café would cover the cost if most people paid little. Pia suggested a "pay it forward" jar on the counter. Kwame said the cost was manageable for a trial.

**Decision:** Trial "pay what you can" soup on Wednesdays from November 2026 to January 2027, with a review at the February meeting.
**Action:** Pia to keep a weekly record of portions served and money received.

## 9. Christmas fair

The Christmas fair will be on Saturday 5 December 2026, 10:00–15:00. Stall fee €25. The aim is to raise €3,000, to go towards rebuilding reserves. Lotte Brenner is coordinating and needs volunteers for the tombola and the café.

**Action:** Lotte to open stall bookings by 1 November.

Agnes Moreau has told the Chair she will step down as a trustee at the AGM in March 2027, after nine years. The board thanked her in her absence.

The board agreed to recruit a new trustee, ideally with finance or accounting experience, to support Kwame.

**Action:** Kwame to draft a role description for the new trustee by the November meeting.
