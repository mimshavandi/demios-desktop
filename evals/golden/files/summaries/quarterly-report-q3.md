# Fernhill Tea Co.: Quarterly report, Q3 2026 (July–September)

Prepared by: Oksana Ferreira, Finance Director
For: Board of Directors, meeting of 21 October 2026
Status: final

---

## 1. Summary

Q3 was a good quarter for sales and a harder one for margins. Revenue grew 9% compared with Q3 2025, helped by a strong online quarter and the launch of our cold-brew range in August. Gross margin fell, mainly because sea freight costs rose sharply in July and August and we chose not to pass all of that on to customers.

Headline figures:

| | Q3 2026 | Q3 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | €2.41m | €2.21m | +9% |
| Gross margin | 41.2% | 43.5% | −2.3 points |
| Operating profit | €182k | €205k | −11% |
| Cash at end of quarter | €1.12m | €0.96m | +17% |
| Headcount (end of quarter) | 64 | 58 | +6 |

We remain on track for the full-year plan on revenue, but about €90k behind plan on operating profit.

## 2. Revenue by channel

| Channel | Q3 2026 | Share | Change vs Q3 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Online shop (direct to customer) | €916k | 38% | +21% |
| Subscriptions | €482k | 20% | +12% |
| Wholesale (cafés, delis, hotels) | €699k | 29% | −4% |
| Own shops (Haldorf and Brückstadt) | €313k | 13% | +3% |
| **Total** | **€2.41m** | **100%** | **+9%** |

**Online** had its best quarter ever. The new product pages and the faster checkout introduced in June increased the conversion rate from 2.1% to 2.6%. Average order value was flat at €34.

**Subscriptions** grew to 9,850 active subscribers at the end of September (8,700 a year earlier). Monthly churn improved to 5.8%, from 6.4% in Q2, after we introduced the option to pause a subscription rather than cancel it. About 1,100 subscribers used the pause option in the quarter; 62% of those who paused in July had restarted by the end of September.

**Wholesale** fell 4%. We lost two hotel accounts in the Brückstadt area to a cheaper competitor in July, worth about €40k a quarter together. On the positive side, we signed 11 new café accounts, mostly small independents, which together should bring around €25k a quarter once they are fully stocked. The wholesale team believes the hotel market will remain price-driven and recommends we focus on independent cafés.

**Own shops** were steady. The Brückstadt shop had building works outside for most of August, which reduced footfall.

## 3. Cold-brew launch

The cold-brew range (three flavours, sold in 250 ml cans and as 1-litre concentrate) launched on 4 August.

- Sales in the first eight weeks: €184k, against a target of €150k.
- The 1-litre concentrate was the surprise success, making up 58% of cold-brew sales, mostly through the online shop.
- The peach and black tea flavour sold best; the hibiscus flavour sold least and we have reduced the next production run.
- Cans have a lower margin (about 31%) than our loose-leaf teas (about 46%) because of packaging and co-packer costs.

Because cold brew is seasonal, we expect sales to drop by roughly half in Q4. The plan is to keep the concentrate on sale all year and bring the cans back in spring.

## 4. Costs and margin

Gross margin fell from 43.5% to 41.2%. The main reasons:

1. **Freight.** Sea freight from our two main growing regions cost about 35% more per container in July and August than a year earlier. This alone took about 1.5 points off gross margin. Rates eased slightly in September.
2. **Product mix.** Cold-brew cans have a lower margin than our core loose-leaf range (see section 3). This took about 0.5 points off.
3. **Currency.** A weaker euro against the currencies we buy in made tea about 2% more expensive. This took about 0.3 points off.

We raised prices on 20 lines by an average of 4% on 1 September. The full effect of that will show in Q4.

Operating expenses rose 8%, mainly because of the six new hires (two in customer service, two in the warehouse, one in marketing and one data analyst) and the marketing spend for the cold-brew launch (€46k).

## 5. Operations

- **Warehouse.** Orders shipped on the same day rose to 94% (from 89% in Q2). Peak days in August stretched the team; we used agency staff on 12 days.
- **Customer service.** Average first response time for emails was 5 hours (target: 4 hours). The two new customer-service hires started in September and response times are already improving.
- **Quality.** One batch of Earl Grey (batch EG-0726) was withdrawn from sale in July after customers reported a musty smell. Around 400 packs were affected; all customers were refunded. The cause was a damp container; we have changed our inspection process at the port.

## 6. Cash and investment

Cash at the end of September was €1.12m, up from €0.96m a year earlier, helped by the subscription growth (customers pay in advance) and tighter control of stock.

The board approved in July an investment of **€350k in warehouse automation** (a conveyor and automatic box-sizing machine). Installation is planned for January 2027, after the Christmas peak. We expect it to cut packing costs by about 15% and allow us to handle Christmas 2027 volumes without agency staff.

No new borrowing was taken out in the quarter. The existing loan of €400k is being repaid on schedule; €260k remains.

## 7. People

Headcount at the end of September was 64 (58 a year ago). Staff turnover in the last 12 months was 9%, the lowest in three years. The staff survey in September had a 78% response rate; 81% of respondents said they would recommend Fernhill as a place to work (up from 74%). The most common complaint was the temperature in the warehouse in summer; we are getting quotes for better ventilation.

## 7a. Marketing

Marketing spend in Q3 was €118k (Q3 2025: €84k), of which €46k went on the cold-brew launch. The rest was split between search advertising (€38k), the loyalty programme (€19k) and trade shows (€15k).

- The cold-brew launch campaign reached an estimated 1.4 million people, mostly through short videos made with three independent cafés that stock our tea.
- Cost per new online customer fell from €19 to €16.
- The loyalty programme now has 21,400 members. Members spend on average 1.7 times as much per year as non-members.
- We stopped advertising on two price-comparison sites in August because the customers they brought rarely ordered again.

For Q4, about 60% of the marketing budget will go towards gift sets and advent calendars, with the rest on subscriptions, which are our most profitable customers over time.

## 7b. Customer feedback

We received 2,930 product reviews in the quarter, with an average rating of 4.6 out of 5 (Q2: 4.5).

- The most praised products were the cold-brew concentrate, the smoked black tea and the "Sleepy Meadow" herbal blend.
- The most common complaints were about delivery times in August (linked to the peak days mentioned in section 5) and about the new compostable pouches tearing at the seal. We have raised the pouch issue with the supplier and a stronger seal is being tested.
- The Earl Grey withdrawal (section 5) led to 63 complaints, nearly all resolved within a week.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Level | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier concentration | High | 62% of our tea (by value) comes from two estates. A bad harvest at either would hurt. We are trialling two new estates in Q4. |
| Freight costs | Medium | Rates have eased but remain volatile. |
| Currency | Medium | We hedge about half of our purchases six months ahead. |
| Wholesale price pressure | Medium | Hotels are moving to cheaper suppliers. See section 2. |
| Warehouse capacity | Low (from Q1 2027) | Should be resolved by the automation project. |

## 9. Outlook for Q4

Q4 is our biggest quarter because of Christmas gift sets and advent calendars.

- **Revenue guidance for Q4: €2.7m to €2.9m** (Q4 2025: €2.62m).
- Advent calendar pre-orders are 18% ahead of last year at the same point.
- Gross margin should recover to around 42.5% as the September price rise takes effect and freight eases, but this depends on freight rates holding.
- We will keep hiring to a minimum until the warehouse automation is in place.

## 10. Decisions requested from the board

1. Approve the trial of two new tea estates (initial orders of up to €60k in total).
2. Note the Q4 guidance.
3. Approve a budget of up to €25k for better warehouse ventilation, to be installed before summer 2027.
